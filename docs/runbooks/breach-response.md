# Breach response runbook

Use this runbook when you suspect or confirm unauthorized access to, loss of, or disclosure of personal information.

## 1. Contain and document

- Stop ongoing unauthorized access or disclosure where you can do so safely.
- Record what you know: what happened, when it was discovered, systems involved, and who is leading the response.

## 2. Assess real risk of significant harm

Under PIPEDA, assess whether the breach creates a **real risk of significant harm** to an individual (see OPC guidance, Part 6). Consider the **sensitivity** of the personal information involved and the **probability** that the information will be misused.

## 3. Report to the Privacy Commissioner of Canada

When the breach creates a real risk of significant harm, report it to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada **as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach occurred** (PIPEDA s. 10.1(2); see OPC guidance, Parts 1 and 2; Breach of Security Safeguards Regulations, s. 2).

- OPC guidance: [What you need to know about mandatory reporting of breaches of security safeguards](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/privacy-breaches-at-your-business/gd_pb_201810/)
- Breach report form: [Report a privacy breach at your business](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/report-a-concern/report-a-privacy-breach-at-your-organization/report-a-privacy-breach-at-your-business/)

## 4. Notify affected individuals

Unless otherwise prohibited by law, notify affected individuals as soon as feasible after you determine that a breach creating a real risk of significant harm has occurred. Notify them directly (in person, by telephone, mail, email, or another form of communication a reasonable person would consider appropriate). Indirect notification is permitted only when direct notification would likely cause further harm to the individual, would likely cause undue hardship for the organization, or when you do not have contact information for the individual (Breach of Security Safeguards Regulations, s. 5(1)); it must then be given by public communication or a similar measure that could reasonably be expected to reach the affected individuals (s. 5(2)). See OPC guidance, Part 4.

## 5. Notify other organizations

When you notify an individual, you must also notify any government institutions or organizations that you believe can reduce the risk of harm that could result from the breach or mitigate that harm (OPC guidance, Part 5). Examples from the OPC include law enforcement (when there is an attack on your computer system and you believe it may be able to reduce or mitigate the harm) and the organization that processes your payments (for a breach affecting payment card information).

## 6. Keep breach records

Keep a record of **every** breach of security safeguards involving personal information under your control, whether or not it poses a real risk of significant harm. Retain each record for **24 months** from the date the breach was determined to have occurred.

- Regulatory text (Canada Gazette, SOR/2018-64 s. 6(1)): [https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2018/2018-04-18/html/sor-dors64-eng.html](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2018/2018-04-18/html/sor-dors64-eng.html)

Use `docs/privacy/breach-record-template.md` for each incident record.
